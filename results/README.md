
# Results

This folder presents the principal findings and analytical outputs of the research project **"Underlying Dimensions and Determinants of Technological Drought in Relatively Uplifted Regions of Bangladesh."**

The study examined farmers' perceptions, irrigation practices, drought-related experiences, and the underlying socioeconomic, environmental, and technological dimensions influencing technological drought in the Madhupur Tract of Bangladesh.

## 1. Respondent Characteristics and Drought Perceptions

The survey results provide insights into farmers' agricultural practices, access to irrigation facilities, drought experiences, and preparedness.

The findings indicate that drought-related impacts are commonly perceived as moderate, while a considerable proportion of respondents reported limited preparedness to cope with drought consequences.

April and May were identified as the most affected months based on respondents' experiences.

## 2. Irrigation Practices and Technology Use

The study examined the use of modern and traditional irrigation methods among farmers.

The findings indicate a substantial reliance on groundwater-based irrigation technologies, particularly:

- Electric shallow tubewells
- Electric deep tubewells
- Diesel-operated deep tubewells
- Diesel-operated shallow tubewells
- Diesel-operated low-lift pumps

Traditional irrigation methods were generally less commonly used, indicating a shift toward modern irrigation technologies.

However, the use of modern irrigation systems does not necessarily ensure reliable water access, as their operation can be constrained by energy shortages, fuel costs, equipment limitations, and financial barriers.

## 3. PCA Diagnostics and Component Extraction

The suitability of the 55 survey variables for Principal Component Analysis was supported by a KMO value of 0.94.

The PCA identified ten principal components, which collectively explained 61.93% of the total variance after rotation.

The extracted components represent different underlying dimensions of technological drought and associated resilience challenges.

## 4. Underlying Dimensions of Technological Drought

The ten extracted components were interpreted in relation to the following broad themes:

1. **PC1 – Technological Options:** Technological options for reducing drought effects.
2. **PC2 – Socioeconomic Resilience:** Socioeconomic factors influencing resilience.
3. **PC3 – Water Management and Irrigation:** Water management practices and irrigation dependency.
4. **PC4 – Energy and Technological Limitations:** Energy provision and constraints affecting technological operation.
5. **PC5 – Environmental and Policy Issues:** Environmental conditions and policy-related constraints.
6. **PC6 – Cooperation and Traditional Knowledge:** Farmer cooperation and integration of traditional knowledge.
7. **PC7 – Access to Technology:** Constraints associated with access to agricultural technologies.
8. **PC8–PC10 – Environmental and Technical Challenges:** Additional environmental problems and failures within the technical system.

## 5. Major Findings

The findings demonstrate that technological drought in the Madhupur Tract is not simply a consequence of insufficient rainfall or environmental water scarcity. It is also associated with limitations in the systems and resources required to access, distribute, and manage water for agricultural activities.

The major challenges identified include:

- Inadequate and unreliable irrigation and water-supply systems.
- Unreliable electricity and fuel availability.
- Financial constraints affecting access to modern irrigation technologies.
- Socioeconomic inequalities among farming households.
- Limited cooperation and knowledge-sharing among farmers.
- Environmental and institutional constraints affecting agricultural water management.

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of considering technological, socioeconomic, environmental, and institutional factors when examining drought-related challenges in smallholder agricultural systems.
